🧩 Key computer terms

CPUThe "brain" of the computer — it processes every instruction and calculation.
RAMTemporary memory that holds data the computer is actively using. More RAM = smoother multitasking.
HardwareThe physical parts you can touch — keyboard, monitor, mouse, motherboard.
SoftwarePrograms and instructions that tell hardware what to do — apps, games, operating systems.
BrowserAn app (Chrome, Firefox, Edge) used to visit websites on the internet.
Operating SystemThe main software that manages the computer — e.g. Windows, Android, Linux.
Storage (ROM/SSD/HDD)Where files are permanently kept, even after the computer is switched off.
Input / Output DevicesInput: keyboard, mouse, mic. Output: monitor, speaker, printer.

Stop 3

🔒 Cyber Safety Awareness

Simple habits that keep students safe while using phones, computers, and social media.

🔑 Weak Passwords

Never use "123456", your name, or your birth date. Use a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols — at least 8 characters.

🎣 Phishing Scams

Fake messages or emails pretending to be your bank or a prize. Never click unknown links or share OTPs with anyone.

📵 Social Media Oversharing

Avoid posting your home address, school timing, or location live. Keep profiles private and only accept known contacts.

🚫 Cyberbullying

Never forward hurtful messages or images. If you or a friend is targeted, save evidence and tell a trusted adult or teacher immediately.

✅ Safe online behaviour checklist

✔ Think before you clickUnknown links can install malware or steal your data.
✔ Verify before you trustCheck the sender and spelling of a website URL before entering details.
✔ Use two-factor authenticationAn extra OTP step keeps your accounts safe even if a password leaks.
✔ Log out of shared devicesAlways sign out on cyber café or shared school computers.

About this Project

Problem Identified

Students at Rajarshi Shahu Maharaj Vidyalay, a government school, had limited exposure to computers, the internet, and online safety practices. Through surveys, interviews, and classroom observation conducted during the internship, low digital literacy and lack of cyber-safety awareness were identified as the core problems.

Solution & Approach

ClickShiksha was built as a simple, free, and reusable awareness resource covering computer literacy, basic programming awareness, cyber safety, and career guidance. The name uses the CLICK acronym: Computer and Cyber Literacy, Learning, Innovation, Career guidance, and Knowledge. Its tagline is "One click towards digital knowledge".

Methodology

The internship began with regular school visits, classroom observation, and interactions with teachers and students to understand the school environment and identify gaps in digital literacy, cyber safety, career awareness, and technical support. Based on these observations, computer literacy, cyber safety awareness, and career guidance sessions were conducted, along with support for the school's technical and record-keeping activities. The insights gained during these activities directly shaped the content and structure of ClickShiksha, a prototype educational web portal developed as a long-term learning resource for students.
